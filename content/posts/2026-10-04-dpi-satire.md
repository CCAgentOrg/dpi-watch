## The Week Everything Was Only an Option

I am the consent form my mother never signed. I survived because I was filed under “not applicable”: the Education Ministry said no data had been shared, UIDAI said it had viewing rights, and I was too young to know that a window can be a data pipeline if the paperwork insists it is architecture.

In September 2026, an RTI reply disclosed that UIDAI could view schoolchildren’s Aadhaar mandatory-biometric-update status on UDISE+, the national school database. The Education Ministry said no data was shared and no agreement had been entered into. The reply did not say what the authority could see, whether the view was of individual pupils or school totals, who could access it, or what legal instrument allowed it. My mother asked which box to tick. The form offered two: “I consent” and “I understand that this is not data-sharing.” She ticked neither. The system recorded her silence as a successful interaction.

We were taught the distinction early. Sharing meant sending a file. Viewing meant opening a file. If you opened it every morning, that was still not sharing; it was a relationship. The Education Ministry said it had no agreement with UIDAI, which was reassuring: the arrangement had achieved interoperability without the inconvenience of agreeing what it was.

Then came the face. UIDAI announced face authentication for the electronic Public Distribution System as a consent-based, contactless *additional* mode, especially for people who struggle with fingerprints. States and Union Territories could integrate it. That is a real potential benefit: a worn fingerprint should not strand a family at the ration shop. The future problem begins only if an additional route becomes the route, or if “consent” is spoken by a queue that has already been told there is no other way. In my 2032 ration line, the camera asked me to smile. I asked whether I could decline. It said, “Certainly. Please authenticate to confirm.”

A clerk later explained that this was not coercion, because the camera had not touched me. Contactless, therefore, was not merely a description of the technology. It was the government’s preferred distance from the consequences.

That same week, India launched the National Digital Tourism Stack with ONDC. The plan is to make tourism providers discoverable across participating platforms, with verified identities and credentials, search, payments, ratings and grievance redressal; a pilot was planned for January 2027. The tourism mission also said visitor data and feedback would help destinations manage footfall against carrying capacity. That may help small providers get found; it also raises questions before visitor data becomes a management tool: who sees it, and can communities contest how “capacity” is defined? A destination is not a spreadsheet with a sunset attachment.

The system’s finest feature was that every concern had a future-facing answer. Who saw the child’s status? “Only authorized viewers.” Who authorized them? “The process is being streamlined.” Could I refuse the face scan? “It is an additional mode.” Could the village refuse to be recommended? “Discovery is platform-neutral.” In the archive, these phrases sit beside the old Aadhaar form, where “voluntary” meant you could say no without losing the service. The curators left a blank space for the next definition.

## What Actually Happened

- **28 September:** MediaNama reported that an RTI reply left the scope and legal basis of UIDAI’s UDISE+ “viewing rights” unclear. It reported child-specific DPDP duties are due from May 2027. [^1]
- **29 September:** UIDAI announced ePDS face authentication as an additional, consent-based mode, alongside SWIK Portal 2.0 and a low-volume authentication framework. [^2]
- **27 September:** Tourism Ministry launched the ONDC-linked tourism stack; a January 2027 pilot is planned. A separate mission proposes using visitor data and feedback in destination management. [^3]

## Practical Citizen Checks

1. **Parents:** Ask the school or Education Ministry what UIDAI can view, whether records are student-level, how records are matched, who has access, and what order governs it. Request the records and access rules by RTI; keep the reply and appeal an incomplete response.
2. **Ration beneficiaries:** Ask which modes your State has enabled and what happens if one fails. Record the shop, date, and details of any denial; use the state food-department grievance route rather than treating a failed match as loss of entitlement.
3. **Tourism communities and small providers:** During the pilot, ask the Ministry, State, or platform what supplier and visitor data are collected, who sees them, how long they are kept, and how residents can contest listings or capacity decisions.
