## The Meeting That Could Not Charge Me

In 2026 I sold cloth at a counter and kept a ledger: purchase price, selling price, margin. It was an old-fashioned interface. When the price changed, someone had to tell me.

That October, a UPI merchant discount rate was scheduled: 0.4% on specified person-to-merchant payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 on payments of ₹75,000 or more. Person-to-person transfers and lower-value merchant payments remained outside it; exempt small merchants were to stay protected. It was a merchant-side charge, not a new fee on a customer's UPI screen.[^1]

A week before the October 15 start, reports said a postponement to January 1 was under consideration. On October 9, the scheduled decision meeting did not happen; no next date or decision was available. The Finance Minister said stakeholders would handle any deferment. I had a stake, but no idea whether I had a seat.[^2][^3]

“Of course you count,” said the fictional clerk at the Office of Stakeholder Inclusion. “You are included in the impact assessment.”

“Can I see the assessment?”

“It is being finalised after the impact.”

The October 15 date remained announced; January 1 and a wider small-merchant exemption were only proposals. Some executives wanted October to hold. I could choose any position, provided someone else discussed it for me.

The Reserve Bank Governor said no fall in UPI volume was visible yet and expected little impact from a small fee. I admired the metric: a payment count can stay magnificent while a shopkeeper absorbs a cost or changes a price. The dashboard counts taps; the counter counts margins.[^4]

Kerala’s textile and garments association warned that the charge could squeeze thin margins, raise prices, and push traders toward cash. A national payment rail should answer with evidence, not a reassuring adjective.[^5]

The national conversation had advanced to “DPI 2.0.” In an October interview, ONDC’s chief described ambitions for user control, interoperability, and AI in local languages. The same report noted authentication errors that cut people off from benefits and unresolved governance questions.[^6] The future would give me control in several languages. I was still waiting for the rate card in one.

### Bureaucratic Testimonial

*“The stakeholder process is fully inclusive. Merchants are represented by the fact that the charge applies to merchants. This is the highest form of participation: being affected without being inconvenienced by a meeting.”* — **Fictional statement, Office of Stakeholder Inclusion, 2031**

The joke is not that a digital rail must be free forever; rails cost money. It is that an infrastructure can promise user control while the person paying the bill infers the rules from meeting reports. “Who decides?” “The stakeholders.” “Who are they?” “The ecosystem.” “Can I see the decision?” “It is interoperable.”

## What Actually Happened This Week

Between October 7 and 9, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra said he did not expect the proposed MDR to materially reduce UPI volumes. Business Standard reported a possible January 1, 2027 rollout and wider small-merchant exemption, both still proposals. The Finance Minister said a deferment would be handled by ecosystem stakeholders; Moneycontrol reported that the October 9 committee meeting did not happen. The Hindu covered Kerala textile merchants’ cost concerns. An October 8 DPI 2.0 interview paired user-control and interoperability ambitions with authentication failures and governance concerns. As of October 11, October 15 remained the announced MDR date, with no formal postponement identified.[^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7]

## Actionable Citizen Rights Advice

1. **If you accept UPI:** Ask your acquiring bank or payment provider, in writing, which merchant category applies to your account, whether an exemption covers you, the applicable rate and cap, the effective date, and how deductions, refunds, and disputes will appear on your settlement statement. Keep the answer and compare it with actual settlements.
2. **If you pay by UPI:** A merchant-side MDR is not itself a customer-side UPI fee. If a separate “UPI surcharge” appears, request an itemised receipt and keep it; raise the issue with the merchant and your app or bank rather than assuming the fee is authorised.
3. **For public accountability:** Ask NPCI and the relevant bank or provider to publish the final date, categories, exemption rules, merchant appeal route, and evidence used to assess effects on small businesses. Treat “under consideration” as a proposal—not a rule—and “volume” as one measure, not the whole public impact.
