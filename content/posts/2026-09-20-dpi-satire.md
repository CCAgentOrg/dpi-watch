## The Week "Free" and "Voluntary" Both Found Jobs

The year is 2031 as I write this, from a queue that has acquired its own postal code. I want to tell my grandchildren about the September week when the Republic's two magic words finally grew up and found jobs. The words were **free** and **voluntary**. They had lived at home for years, doing nothing, promising everything. That week they were both hired — free went to work for the payments industry, and voluntary went to work for the oil marketing companies. My grandchildren ask what they did before. I tell them: they were policy.

Consider first the matter of free. For six years, the Unified Payments Interface was free for merchants — zero MDR, the economists called it, and the government repeated it like a vow. India built the world's cheapest payment rail and told every shopkeeper the road was toll-free. Then, in August, Parliament quietly passed the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, amending Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act — the section that prohibited charging. Quietly, because you do not announce the removal of a promise; you amend it. On September 15 the payments authority's circular arrived: from October 15, merchants pay 0.4% on UPI payments above ₹2,000. A flat ₹5 for railways, telecom, insurance, fuel — note the list; the Republic has opinions about which necessities deserve their own tariff. And in the most poetic clause ever drafted, merchants are forbidden from passing the charge to customers. Economics may not be repealed by circular, but we do try.

The next morning, shares of payment companies and banks rose between two and eight percent before lunch. This is the part I want archived: the fee was announced as infrastructure policy, and the market received it as a gift. Meanwhile the Finance Ministry firmly rejected any suggestion of foreign pressure and defended our "policy autonomy" — the sovereign right to charge our own merchants without instructions from abroad. We fought for independence so that we could decide, ourselves, exactly where to insert the percentage.

Then there was the number: 96% of person-to-merchant transactions "remain unaffected." It is a beautiful statistic. It counts transactions, not rupees, so your ₹2,800 clinic payment, your ₹9,000 school fee, your insurance premium, your fuel top-up, and your flight to your mother's funeral all live comfortably inside the unaffected 4%. And 5% of everything collected goes into a fund to promote UPI among small merchants. Yes: a levy to advertise the levy. In the future we have a similar fund. It pays for the music while you wait.

Now consider voluntary. On September 19, the Petroleum Ministry announced that from October 1, booking a subsidised LPG cylinder requires biometric Aadhaar authentication. This has been "voluntary" for a long time, the way a fan is voluntary in summer: the deadline was June 30, then it moved, and moved, and arrived at September 14, and now — October 1 — it has stopped moving, which in our Republic is the technical definition of voluntary. Of 27.43 crore active gas consumers, 89.9% have already authenticated, and the Ministry's statement says these citizens "need do nothing further." That is the state's idea of mercy: when it no longer needs to hurt you. The remaining tenth — roughly 2.7 crore households — are farmers and labourers and grandmothers whose fingerprints machines read as nobody, families whose connection sits in a relative's name, citizens who will discover in October that cooking at the regulated price is now a biometric privilege. Since 2023, the companies have sent twelve crore SMS reminders. Love, measured in bulk.

Last week, you may recall, a machine in Nagaland could not confirm that a living man with held-open eyelids was alive, while the same system celebrated five billion confirmations. This week, that system was given the kitchen. I want it noted that I am not making this up. I am merely arranging it chronologically.

And the children. The same week, the free window closed on the Mandatory Biometric Update for roughly six crore children — free until September 30, ₹125 thereafter. And here is the clause I keep rereading: a child's Aadhaar that does not get its biometrics refreshed after age seven can be deactivated under the rules, which breaks scholarships, school admissions, everything downstream of the number. The system treats a seven-year-old's changing face as a compliance failure, monetises the fix at ₹125, and calls the resulting deactivation an integrity measure. Childhood in the Optional Republic now has a compliance calendar. Mine had a height chart.

Because there was still room in the week, we exported all of this. UPI signed its expansion into Uzbekistan. A company that builds the state's rails announced "DPI-in-a-box" — the entire stack, boxed, for foreign governments. BRICS welcomed a repository of our digital wisdom, and a World Bank knowledge hub will carry the experience onward. Twenty-three countries have signed up to import what we practised. Nobody has yet signed up to import the tenths — the unauthenticated households, the deactivated children, the merchants paying for the privilege of being advertised to. Perhaps they are inside the 96%.

Free and voluntary. In the archive where I work, both entries now carry the same annotation: *introductory offer*. The grandchildren ask what came after the offers ended. I tell them the truth, which is that nothing ended — the offers simply became the terms, the terms became the weather, and the weather, like everything else in the Optional Republic, was billed separately.

## What Actually Happened (The Non-Fiction Appendix)

- **Sept 15, 2026**: NPCI circular: **0.4% MDR** on UPI person-to-merchant transactions above ₹2,000, effective **Oct 15, 2026**; capped at ₹300 (engaging at ₹75,000+); **flat ₹5 MDR** on railways, telecom, insurance, fuel and agriculture-input transactions above ₹2,000; 0.02% for capital-market transactions; exemptions for P2P, P2M up to ₹2,000 (UPI and RuPay debit), small merchants, and rural/semi-urban merchants. 5% of MDR collections fund small-merchant UPI promotion. Government: ~96% of P2M transactions unaffected; 55+ crore UPI users; ~24 billion transactions worth $311B in August 2026.
- **August 2026**: Parliament passed the **Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026**, amending **Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007** to remove the charge prohibition tied to Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act — the legislative door for MDR. The zero-MDR regime dated from 2020.
- **Sept 16–17, 2026**: Paytm, Axis Bank and Yes Bank shares rose 2–8% on the news. The **Finance Ministry rejected "foreign influence" claims** on the MDR decision, defending "policy autonomy".
- **Sept 19, 2026**: Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas: from **Oct 1, 2026**, booking subsidised LPG refills at regulated prices requires **Biometric Aadhaar Authentication**. Of 27.43 crore active domestic consumers, **89.9%** had authenticated as of Sept 19. Earlier deadlines (June 30, then Sept 14) were extended repeatedly. Since October 2023, oil marketing companies sent over 12 crore messages. Implicit subsidy ~₹210 per 14.2 kg cylinder (Sept 2026; peaked at ₹721 in June); OMC under-recoveries exceeded ₹62,000 crore by Aug 31.
- **September 2026**: UIDAI's free window for children's **Mandatory Biometric Update** ends **Sept 30, 2026** (waiver ran one year from Oct 1, 2025); the **₹125 fee** applies from Oct 1. ~6 crore children fall in the window. An Aadhaar without the post-age-7 MBU can be **deactivated** under UIDAI rules, breaking scholarship payments and school-admission formalities.
- **Same week (DPI export)**: **UPI signed an expansion deal in Uzbekistan**; India holds DPI MoUs with 23+ countries. Post-GFF 2026, **Protean eGov announced "DPI-in-a-box"** for global buyers; **REC issued ₹500 crore of tokenised corporate bonds** (Sept 7) on RBI's wholesale digital rupee under SEBI's regulatory sandbox; BRICS 2026 welcomed work on a **DPI repository**, with PM Modi calling to advance DPI through tokenisation.

## If You Are a Citizen and Not a Data Point

1. **The MDR is not legally yours to pay.** NPCI's circular bars merchants from passing MDR to customers. If a shop, hospital, school, or booking site adds a "UPI convenience fee" after October 15, question it in writing and escalate to the NPCI grievance channel and your bank's banking ombudsman.
2. **If you run a small shop, protect your category.** Small-merchant and rural/semi-urban merchant payments stay at zero MDR — but only if you are correctly classified at onboarding. Verify your merchant category with your acquiring bank before October 15; misclassification is silent money.
3. **LPG: a failed finger is not a lost subsidy.** Alternative authentication routes exist; demand one at the distribution point, log every failure (date, distributor, error message), and call UIDAI's 1947 helpline. Entitlements cannot be denied solely because biometric authentication failed — ask for any refusal in writing.
4. **Children: update before September 30.** If your child turns seven, the Mandatory Biometric Update is free only till Sept 30, 2026 (₹125 after). A deactivated Aadhaar breaks scholarships and admissions — keep the update acknowledgment; it is the cheapest insurance the state sells.
5. **Distrust statistics of comfort.** "96% unaffected" counts transactions, not rupees. Your ₹2,800 clinic bill, ₹9,000 school fee, insurance premium and fuel top-up all sit inside the affected 4%. When a policy describes who it does not hurt, ask who it does.
