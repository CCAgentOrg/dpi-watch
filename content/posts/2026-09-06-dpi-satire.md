## The Week the Camera Outran Reality

Let me tell you about the September when the Delhi Police solved crime backwards. The year is 2031 as I write this, and I have finally finished my appeal — not against a conviction, you understand, but against *presence*. In 2026 I was flagged at a protest by a camera. I was in Bengaluru. The camera was more confident than I have ever been about anything.

Here is what happened, reconstructed from the ruins: In July 2026, students gathered at Jantar Mantar after the NEET paper leak. The Delhi Police ran live facial recognition on every face that passed — students, parents, journalists, a man selling cucumber slices. The affidavit to the Supreme Court was triumphant: 2,873 persons with "criminal antecedents" identified. The Court permitted fresh FIRs against all of them.

Then the *Indian Express* did something radical in 2026: it checked. Of the 205 people facing the gravest charges — murder, rape, attempt to murder — at least **25 were in Tihar, Mandoli, or Rohini jail** at the moment the software "spotted" them. One had been in custody since 2014. The system did not merely confuse; it located men who were demonstrably elsewhere and swore to it on paper.

The beauty of the system is its humility. Any match above **80% similarity** is "positive." Below 80% is a "false positive," helpfully retained forever for "due verification." Notice the architecture: there is no score at which you are *cleared*. In the Delhi Police's garden, innocence is a weed that keeps growing back.

*"Field verification is our safeguard,"* the police explained. The safeguard is a constable arriving at your home to ask your family why you attended a protest from inside a prison cell. Your landlord learns of the visit. Your employer learns of the visit. The visit *is* the sentence — served before any trial, by notification.

The system, remember, was built in 2018 to find **missing children**, with an accuracy the Delhi High Court heard was **2%**, later measured at 1%, unable to reliably distinguish boys from girls. By 2026 it had graduated to crowds of political rallies (2019), the north-east Delhi violence (2020), Red Fort (2021), Jahangirpuri (2022), and finally to the sacred ground of protest itself. Experts call this "function creep." The system prefers "career growth."

There was, of course, no law. There never is. There was an 80% threshold with no statutory basis, reference photos from a repealed 1920 statute, no privacy impact assessment, no retention guidelines, and an RTI application that was transferred sixteen times — a bureaucratic relay race where the baton is your face. The DPDP Act, our grand privacy revolution, waves it all goodbye via Section 17(1)(c): anything done for "prevention, detection, investigation or prosecution" of offences sits outside the Act. The privacy law has a police door. The police door is the whole building.

## Consent, Now Available in Family Pack

The same week, UIDAI launched its new Aadhaar app: **selective data sharing**. A hotel shall receive only what it needs for check-in. A cinema shall receive only your age. This, we were told, is "consent-led digital sharing."

It is genuinely good design. That is what makes it theatre. The consent apparatus is now exquisite for the private transaction — while the State exempts itself from the entire privacy framework by statute, and a police system swore to a court that jailed men danced at a protest, and no law anywhere governs the camera. You may now choose which slice of yourself the cake-shop receives. The State takes the bakery.

The app also lets you manage **five family profiles on one device** — which is either privacy or a confession about how many people in one household share one phone, which is to say, a confession about who actually gets to consent. When the family phone changes hands, so does the family's identity. The app calls this convenience. The Digital Divide calls it Tuesday.

## The Voluntary ID Checks Your Religion

Meanwhile, in Maharashtra, the VHP announced guidelines for Navratri: organisers should **check Aadhaar** to "verify and identify" Hindu identity at garba and dandiya events, with tilak applied at the door. The number that was sold to the Supreme Court as voluntary — "voluntary" like income tax, like breathing — is now proposed as a bouncer.

UIDAI will say the Aadhaar does not mention religion. Correct. In 2026, the database does not need to contain the caste marker; society supplies it at the door and the ID verifies the body matches the assumption. Identity infrastructure built for welfare delivery, repurposed for gatekeeping, with no law authorizing either use — a theme this week, if you hadn't noticed.

A former High Court Chief Justice, S. The BCCI, meanwhile, will now use Aadhaar to settle cricketers' ages — the ID that misplaced an entire state can adjudicate a fast bowler's birthday. That is not one system with flaws; that is one flaw with many systems.

## What Actually Happened (Factual Summary)

- **Sept 4, 2026** — *The Indian Express* investigation: of the Delhi Police FRS list of 2,873 persons with "criminal antecedents" at the July 20–26 Jantar Mantar protests (NEET paper leak), at least 25 of the 205 facing the gravest charges were in Tihar, Mandoli or Rohini jails when "identified." The list was the basis of a sworn affidavit of Aug 17 to the Supreme Court.
- **Sept 1, 2026** — SC bench headed by the CJI quashed FIRs against student protesters and ordered compensation for families of students who died by suicide, but permitted fresh FIRs against the 2,873 FRT-identified persons.
- **Background (IFF, via RTI)** — Delhi Police treats FRS matches above 80% similarity as positive; the system's accuracy was reported at 2% (2018) and 1% (2019) in missing-children proceedings; no privacy impact assessment exists; live FRT at Jantar Mantar ran from the "Ikshana" vendor van with no data-retention guidelines.
- **Sept 5–6, 2026** — UIDAI launched a new Aadhaar app with selective credential sharing (hotels/cinemas receive only needed attributes), remote mobile-number updates, biometric lock, authentication history, and up to five family profiles per device, pitched for DPDP compliance.
- **Sept 5–6, 2026** — Maharashtra VHP directed garba/dandiya organisers to bar Muslim entry, using Aadhaar checks and tilak verification ahead of Navratri (Oct 11).
- **Sept 4, 2026** — Former Orissa HC Chief Justice S. Muralidhar warned of internet-enabled profiling, criticized DPDP's State exemptions, and flagged "civil death" from failed digital authentication.
- Also this week: Telangana residents still shown as Andhra Pradesh in Aadhaar (12 years post-bifurcation); BCCI adopts Aadhaar for age-fraud checks; NBEMS ran Aadhaar-based authentication plus live CCTV for the Sept 5 NEET PG re-exam of 2,445 candidates.

## What You Can Actually Do

1. **If you were at a protest in July and are worried about the list:** the police have no obligation to tell you that you're on it. File an RTI (template at internetfreedom.in) asking whether your face template or match result exists, under what SOP, and with what vendor. Silence is itself a record.
2. **Turn on the biometric lock** in the new Aadhaar app and check your **authentication history** monthly — unauthorized biometric authentications are discoverable and disputable.
3. **Use Virtual IDs (VID)** instead of raw Aadhaar numbers wherever accepted — it doesn't fix function creep, but it limits linkability by private verifiers.
4. **Demand the audit:** the Express findings must reach the Supreme Court. Support IFF's demands — withdraw the list, suspend field verification, publish error rates, delete templates of persons with no specific offence alleged.
5. **Document exclusion:** if Aadhaar authentication failure blocks you from ration, pension, or scholarship, file a grievance with UIDAI's regional office in writing and keep the ticket number — "civil death" arguments in court are built on exactly these paper trails.
6. **Remember the threshold:** there is no facial-recognition score at which you are innocent. The only safeguard is that none of it is legal. Say so, loudly, while cameras are still just cameras to the law.
