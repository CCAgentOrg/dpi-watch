## From the Memoirs of a Future Citizen

It was the week of July 2026. Digital India turned eleven. Confetti rained from PIB press releases. UPI had processed 24,161.69 crore transactions worth ₹314.23 lakh crore in a single financial year. 55.49 crore users onboarded. Twelve countries had adopted it. Narendra Modi called it \"a global benchmark.\"\n\nMeanwhile, in a village in Jharkhand, a man named Suresh discovered that someone had cloned his fingerprint and withdrawn ₹47,000 from his bank account through Aadhaar-enabled Payment System (AePS). He was one of 6 lakh people who had filed complaints about AePS biometric fraud. The fastest-growing crime in India wasn't cybercrime. It was *government-infrastructure crime* — using the system built to include you to drain you.\n\nSuresh didn't read about UPI's global benchmark status that week. He was too busy trying to get a refund under the RBI's three-day rule, which sounds generous until you realize it requires a working internet connection, a functioning bank branch, and a police station that takes your FIR seriously. In Suresh's district, two of those three things were aspirational.\n\nThis is the India that 24,000 crore transactions built. An India where the system works so well for most people that the government can afford to ignore the people it fails spectacularly.\n\n---

## The Offline Paradox

But the government had a solution for people like Suresh: offline UPI.\n\nNPCI announced it was developing an NFC-based tap-to-pay system that would let you make UPI payments without internet. \"A revolutionary step for financial inclusion,\" they said. \"Payments without connectivity.\"\n\nLet me pause and let that sink in. The government's solution to the digital divide was... another digital solution. One that required an NFC-enabled smartphone. The very device that people in the digital divide didn't have. It was like offering a life jacket to a drowning man, but the life jacket required you to already know how to swim.\n\nAnd there was another layer. The offline UPI system was reported to be Aadhaar-biometric-based. Yes — the same biometric infrastructure that had enabled 6 lakh fraud complaints. The same fingerprints that were being cloned. The government's answer to \"our biometric system is being exploited\" was \"let's make it work offline too, so there's even less oversight.\"\n\nBrilliant. Absolutely brilliant.\n\n---

## ONDC: The Network Where Nobody Shows Up

Then there was ONDC — the Open Network for Digital Commerce, the government's grand plan to \"democratize e-commerce\" and break the monopoly of Amazon and Flipkart.\n\nThis week, ONDC celebrated some genuinely impressive numbers: 7 lakh sellers onboarded, presence in 1,200+ cities, and over 150 million transactions completed. Press releases called it \"accelerated adoption.\" An industry expert said it was \"redefining digital commerce.\"\n\nBut here's the thing about ONDC that nobody in the press releases mentioned: 150 million transactions sounds like a lot until you remember that UPI does roughly that many transactions every three days. Amazon India does in a quarter what ONDC has done since launch. The target of 50-60 lakh daily orders by 2026 remained exactly that — a target. Actual daily orders were 5-6 lakh. A tenfold gap between aspiration and reality.\n\nThe ONDC dream was beautiful: small kirana shops, freed from platform monopolies, selling directly to consumers through an open protocol. The ONDC reality was more complicated. Sellers reported delivery delays, data privacy concerns, and the eternal question: \"I'm on ONDC, but where are the buyers?\"\n\nONDC had built the plumbing. It forgot to build the demand. It was the world's most sophisticated empty restaurant. The kitchen works. The menu is printed. The waitstaff is trained. But nobody's walking through the door because all the customers are still at the Amazon across the street, where the app works better, the delivery is faster, and — here's the cruel part — the prices are often the same.\n\nThe government's solution to platform monopoly was to build another platform. You don't break a monopoly by creating a network that needs the monopoly's customers to notice it exists.\n\n---

## Consent by the Numbers

But the quietest story of the week was the most revealing.\n\nA report surfaced that Aadhaar seeding errors — linking your Aadhaar to the wrong bank account or to multiple accounts — accounted for about 18% of payment failures. 18%. Nearly one in five Aadhaar-linked payments failed because the system couldn't correctly match an identity to an account.\n\nThis is the infrastructure that handles 24,000 crore UPI transactions. The identity layer has an 18% error rate on seeding alone. And yet, every new government service — from Census 2027 to digital health records to the newest welfare scheme — defaults to Aadhaar linkage.\n\nThe government's approach to identity was like building a 100-story skyscraper on a foundation with an 18% crack rate. You don't fix the foundation. You just build higher and hope nobody notices the wobbling.\n\n---

## The 12-Country Export

And oh, the celebration of UPI's global expansion. Twelve countries now linked. France, Singapore, UAE. Diplomats posed for photos. PIB called it \"India's digital payments revolution continues to inspire the world.\"\n\nWhat they didn't mention is that India was exporting a payment system whose underlying identity infrastructure had an 18% seeding error rate, whose biometric layer was being cloned for fraud, and whose offline solution required the very connectivity it was supposed to replace.\n\nIndia was exporting the *story* of digital public infrastructure. Whether the infrastructure itself was ready for export was a question nobody asked, because the story was too good.\n\n---

## What Was Actually Happening (Factual Summary)\n\n1. **UPI processed 24,161.69 crore transactions worth ₹314.23 lakh crore in FY 2025-26**, with 55.49 crore users onboarded as of June 2026. The system expanded to 12 countries and captured nearly 49% of global real-time payment volumes. Digital India completed 11 years on July 1, 2026, with the government hailing Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, and CoWIN as global models.\n\n2. **AePS (Aadhaar-enabled Payment System) fraud continues to be the fastest-growing biometric crime in India.** Over 6 lakh complaints were logged at the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (NCRP) in 2024 alone. Fraud occurs when biometrics are silently captured and replayed on AePS terminals to withdraw cash. The RBI's Customer Liability Framework (2017) mandates 100% refund if reported within 3 working days, but enforcement remains uneven.\n\n3. **NPCI is developing NFC-based offline UPI**, allowing payments up to ₹2,000 without internet. Reports suggest the system may be Aadhaar-biometric-based, raising security concerns given existing AePS vulnerabilities. Full rollout expected by end of 2026.\n\n4. **ONDC crossed 150 million cumulative transactions with 7+ lakh sellers across 1,200+ cities.** However, actual daily orders remain at 5-6 lakh against a target of 50-60 lakh — a 10x gap. Grocery and F&B account for over 50% of orders. Sellers report challenges including delivery delays, data privacy concerns, and buyer discovery gaps.\n\n5. **Aadhaar seeding errors account for ~18% of payment failures**, where Aadhaar is linked to the wrong bank account or to multiple accounts, causing direct benefit transfers and payments to fail or misroute.\n\n---

## Your Rights (This Is Not Satire)\n\n- **Lock your Aadhaar biometrics immediately.** Visit uidai.gov.in or use the mAadhaar app. Locking biometrics prevents AePS authentication using your fingerprints or iris. This is the single most effective defense against AePS fraud.\n\n- **If you're an AePS fraud victim, act within 3 working days.** File a complaint with your bank, then with the NPCI dispute resolution portal, then an FIR. The RBI's liability framework mandates full refund if reported within 3 days.\n\n- **Check your Aadhaar seeding status.** Verify which bank account your Aadhaar is linked to via the UIDAI website. An 18% error rate means there's a real chance your subsidies are going to someone else's account.\n\n- **ONDC sellers: read the fine print.** Understand commission structures, delivery logistics, and data sharing terms before onboarding. \"Open network\" does not mean \"no risk.\"\n\n- **You don't have to link everything to Aadhaar.** Supreme Court rulings restrict mandatory Aadhaar to government subsidies, PAN, and income tax. Private entities must offer alternatives.\n\nIndia's DPI stack processed ₹314 lakh crore last year. It also failed to protect 6 lakh people from biometric theft, misrouted 18% of seeded payments, and built an offline solution for people who don't have the device it requires.\n\nThe infrastructure is real. The gaps are real. And the people falling through those gaps are not statistics in a PIB press release.\n\nThey're Suresh from Jharkhand. And he's still waiting for his ₹47,000 back.\n\n---
