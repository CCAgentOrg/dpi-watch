## The Week Permission Got a Head Office

The week I remember — I keep it archived, as one keeps everything now, in a locker nobody has ever seen the inside of — began on November 13, 2026. That was the day the consent managers came into existence. Not consent. Consent managers.

The registration framework under the DPDP Act took effect that Friday; the next day, the Secretary of the Ministry of Electronics and IT inaugurated a portal, dpbi.gov.in, through which companies could apply to become custodians of the nation's permission.[^2] The requirements were exacting and entirely institutional: an Indian company, adequate technical and operational capacity, and a net worth of at least two crore rupees. Your permission, it turns out, requires working capital.[^2]

A Consent Manager, the press release explained, would let citizens "give, manage, review and withdraw consent" through an accessible, transparent and interoperable platform, and would be "accountable to the Data Principals."[^2] I paused at that sentence the way one pauses at a beautifully printed menu outside a restaurant that has not yet been licensed to cook. Because two lines further down, the same release said so itself: the rights of Data Principals, the obligations of companies, the Board's power to hold inquiries and impose penalties — all scheduled for May 13, 2027.[^2]

Six months between the registry of choice and the right to choose. Six months in which a citizen could, in principle, appoint a licensed custodian for decisions they were not yet legally entitled to make.

"It's like opening invigilation centres before the exam is notified," I said to a fictional founder of a fictional consent manager, in the year she became rich.

"You're reading it backwards," she said. "We're not the plumbing for rights. Rights are the marketing for plumbing."

"And until May 2027?"

"Until May 2027, accountability is aspirational. It keeps everyone's blood pressure down."

## The Machine That Requires Eyes

The same week — the state does enjoy a good simultaneous launch — the Supreme Court lost patience with a different piece of the future. A bench headed by Justice J.B. Pardiwala warned that it would consider contempt proceedings if the government did not finally answer, properly, an application filed by a blind advocate named Amar Jain.[^1]

The application concerned face authentication. The new Aadhaar app, it said, requires the applicant's eyes "to be attentive and looking into the frame." Persons with blindness cannot complete it. That would be an inconvenient footnote, except that e-KYC — the gate through which banks, telecom and markets process a citizen — had come to rely only on face authentication.[^1] Mr. Jain annexed his own latest rejection: a private bank declining his credit card e-KYC in October 2026.[^1] In 2025, the same court had ruled in Pragya Prasun that digital access must accommodate persons with disabilities; the system's response, one year on, was a fresh rejection letter.[^1]

There is a particular cruelty in a machine that demands you look at it. The state built a test of presence that fails the blind, wired it to banking, and called the result inclusion. Aliveness, we had already learned, can be renewed by face. Now presence, too, must be performed with eyes. The frame is not checking whether you are you. It is checking whether you can attend.

And while the court waited for its reply, the kitchen waited too. A separate application before the same bench challenges the Petroleum Ministry's decision, announced by press release in September, to make biometric Aadhaar authentication compulsory for subsidised LPG refills from October 1, 2026.[^1] Compulsory. Aadhaar, under the amendment of 2019, is voluntary, and no service may be denied for refusing it. The word compulsory has now been asked to explain itself to the Supreme Court. Historically, it does not answer; it is reissued.

## Consent, a Subordinate Clause

Meanwhile in Mumbai, the National Cancer Grid was holding its annual meeting, and the Union Health Secretary was announcing numbers that sounded like astronomical events: ninety-nine crore ABHA IDs created; roughly a hundred crore health records linked; a hundred and fifty-three crore cancer screenings at 1.87 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs — 37.1 crore for oral cancer, 17.4 crore for breast, 9.7 crore for cervical.[^3]

The screening records of cancer patients, she said, should be linked with their ABHA IDs, for continuity of care. And then, glancing briefly in the direction of the century's most important word: "With patients' consent, health records should be accessible to healthcare providers at different levels of care." And again: data, "with patients' consent," could be used to advance cancer care and research.[^3]

I want to be fair. The care is real; continuity of records saves lives; no satire should deny a cancer patient her file when she changes cities. But notice what the grammar has done. Consent is no longer the sentence. It is the subordinate clause, tucked after the comma, agreeing with whatever the main clause has already decided. A hundred crore records linked — subject. With patients' consent — modifier, optional in feel, load-bearing in law.

By 2031, the comma had been given its own department.

### Bureaucratic Testimonial

*"Registration is not delayed implementation; it is early faith. The machinery of permission stands ready, staffed, and profitable. The permission itself is expected in May, or when convenient, whichever is later. Citizens unable to look into the frame may state their consent in writing, provided they can see the form."* — **Fictional statement, Registered Consent Custodians Association, Ministry of Subordinate Clauses, 2031**

## Buy Now, Consent Later

One more exhibit from the same week, because the future is generous with exhibits. ONDC, the open network for digital commerce, reported that lending on the network had crossed four hundred crore rupees, and announced plans for business loans and buy-now-pay-later products.[^4] The utopian network of small sellers had located its growth market: debt.

I do not begrudge a kirana her credit. But consider the sequencing the country was taught that week: the infrastructure of consent first, the rights second; the face check first, the accessible alternative later; the records linked now, the consent in the subordinate clause; the purchase now, the payment later. India was not building digital public infrastructure. It was teaching an entire economy the same syntax: do the thing; schedule the permission.

## Factual Summary of the Events Satirized

Four real events from the week of November 13–22, 2026 anchor this piece: (1) the Consent Manager registration framework under the DPDP Act came into effect on November 13, 2026, and on November 14 MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan launched the dpbi.gov.in registration portal; applicants must be Indian companies with net worth of at least ₹2 crore, Consent Managers are to be accountable to Data Principals, and the release confirms that Data Principal rights, Data Fiduciary obligations, and the Board's inquiry and penalty processes take effect on May 13, 2027;[^2] (2) on November 16, a Supreme Court bench headed by Justice J.B. Pardiwala warned of contempt if the Union and authorities did not give a detailed response to an application by advocate Amar Jain, who is blind, stating that the new Aadhaar app's face authentication "requires eyes to be attentive and looking into the frame," that e-KYC relies only on face authentication, and annexing his October 2026 credit-card e-KYC rejection by a private bank; the application cites non-implementation of the 2025 Pragya Prasun v. Union of India judgment, and the Court also sought a response on a separate application challenging the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas's mandate making biometric Aadhaar authentication compulsory for subsidised domestic LPG refills from October 1, 2026;[^1] (3) at the National Cancer Grid Annual Meeting 2026 in Mumbai, Union Health Secretary Punya Salila Srivastava reported ~99 crore ABHA IDs, ~100 crore linked health records and 153 crore cancer screenings at 1.87 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, and urged linking cancer screening records to ABHA IDs, with health records accessible "with patients' consent" and data usable, "with patients' consent," for care and research;[^3] and (4) ONDC reported network lending above ₹400 crore with plans for business loans and BNPL products (reported November 19).[^4] The "voluntary vs compulsory" tension is legal-historical framing: the Aadhaar and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 established voluntary authentication with prohibitions on service denial. ## Actionable Citizen Rights Advice

- **If face authentication fails you (or someone you know):** You are entitled to alternatives. Ask the bank or service provider, in writing, for operator-assisted or in-person e-KYC under the Pragya Prasun (2025) directions; escalate to the RBI Ombudsman (CMS portal) with the rejection reference. For Aadhaar itself, use a UIDAI Seva Kendra or the 1947 helpline and file a grievance citing accessibility. Documented complaints also feed the pending Supreme Court matter — keep dated copies.
- **For the LPG biometric mandate:** If a distributor refuses a subsidised refill because you decline or cannot complete biometric authentication, demand the alternative-ID route in writing and complain through your OMC's (IOC/BPC/HPC) grievance channel. The mandate is under challenge in the Supreme Court; dated refusal records from citizens are what such cases run on.
- **For DPDP consent managers:** The list of registered Consent Managers will be published on the Data Protection Board's website. Until May 13, 2027, withdrawal of consent has limited teeth — minimise what you share, screenshot consent screens, and after May 13 use a registered Consent Manager to review and withdraw systematically.
- **For ABHA / health records:** Linkage is meant to be consent-based. At any facility you can ask what is being linked to your ABHA ID, request the consent artefact, refuse linkage, and use ABDM's grievance mechanism for unauthorised linkage. Families managing a cancer patient's records should confirm at each hospital which records move and to whom.
- **Always:** Get refusals in writing with the exact reason — "face authentication failed" is a different refusal than "document mismatch." A dated, written refusal converts an exclusion into evidence.
