# UPI 101: India's Real-Time Payment System

## What is UPI?

The **Unified Payments Interface (UPI)** is an instant, real-time payment system developed by the **National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)** under the regulatory oversight of the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)**. Launched on **11 April 2016** by then-RBI Governor Dr. Raghuram G. Rajan, UPI enables users to transfer money between any two bank accounts using a mobile device — 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year — using only a **Virtual Payment Address (VPA)** or UPI ID (e.g., `name@bank`). [^1][^2]

UPI sits on top of the **Immediate Payment Service (IMPS)**, leveraging existing banking rails for settlement while providing a dramatically simpler user experience. It is **open-source at the API level**, meaning any bank or regulated entity can build on it, and it is **interoperable** — a PhonePe user can pay a Google Pay user at any bank, seamlessly. [^3]

UPI has become the **dominant retail payment instrument in India**, accounting for an estimated **57% of total payment transactions** in the country, surpassing cash (38%) as of a Finance Ministry-commissioned study. [^4] As of July 2026, UPI processes over **23.6 billion transactions** per month valued at nearly **₹30 lakh crore**, with **741 banks** live on the network. [^5]

## How It Works

### The Architecture

UPI operates as a **centralised switch** managed by NPCI that connects all participating banks. The key participants are:

1. **Payment Service Provider (PSP) Banks** — Banks that provide UPI apps to customers (e.g., SBI YONO, iMobile Pay by ICICI)
2. **Third-Party Application Providers (TPAPs)** — Non-bank apps built on bank PSP rails (e.g., PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm, Amazon Pay)
3. **Remitter Bank** — The sender's bank, which debits the account
4. **Beneficiary Bank** — The receiver's bank, which credits the account
5. **NPCI** — The central switch that routes and settles transactions

### Transaction Flow

1. A user initiates a payment (push) or collect request (pull) through a UPI app
2. The TPAP/PSP app sends the request to its **PSP bank**
3. The PSP bank routes it to **NPCI's central switch**
4. NPCI routes the request to the **beneficiary's PSP bank**
5. The beneficiary's bank credits the account and sends an acknowledgement back through the chain
6. NPCI settles the transaction between banks via the **NFS (National Financial Switch)**

The entire process typically completes in **under 3 seconds**. [^3]

### Key Features

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Virtual Payment Address (VPA)** | A single, bank-agnostic identifier (e.g., `name@oksbi`) — no need to share account numbers or IFSC codes |
| **Push & Pull** | Send money (push) or request money (collect, now discontinued for P2P) |
| **QR Code Payments** | Scan-to-pay at merchants using interoperable Bharat QR codes |
| **UPI AutoPay (Mandates)** | Recurring payments for subscriptions, EMIs, bill payments with user consent |
| **UPI Lite** | Small-value offline payments (up to ₹500 per transaction, ₹2,000 wallet balance) without PIN entry |
| **Credit on UPI** | Pre-approved credit lines from banks accessible directly through UPI apps |
| **UPI 123PAY** | UPI for feature phones via IVR, missed call, and app-based methods — no internet required |
| **Invoice-in-Box** | Merchants can send invoices directly through UPI apps |
| **Signed Intent & QR** | Enhanced security for high-value transactions with pre-validated payment intents |
| **Tap & Pay (NFC)** | Contactless UPI payments at NFC-enabled terminals |

### UPI for Feature Phones: UPI 123PAY

Launched in **March 2022**, UPI 123PAY enables the approximately **400 million feature phone users** in India to access UPI without a smartphone or internet connection. It operates through four methods: [^6]

- **IVR (Interactive Voice Response)** — Call a designated number, follow voice prompts
- **Missed Call** — Give a missed call, receive a callback to complete the transaction
- **App-based** — Limited UPI functionality on feature phone apps (developed by Airtel Payments Bank, Jio)
- **Sound-based** — Audio signals between phones for proximity payments

Transaction limit: **₹5,000 per transaction** via UPI 123PAY. [^6]

### UPI Lite: Offline Small Payments

Launched in **September 2022**, UPI Lite creates an on-device wallet loaded from the linked bank account. It enables:

- Payments up to **₹500 per transaction** without requiring a PIN
- Wallet balance up to **₹2,000** at any time
- Works even with poor or no network connectivity (low-bandwidth mode)
- **Auto top-up** feature added subsequently for automatic refilling

As of CY2024, UPI Lite transactions saw a **4x growth** in monthly volume. [^7]

## Layers Classification (L1-L7)

Based on the IndiaStack Layers framework, UPI occupies the following positions:

| Layer | Classification | UPI's Role |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 — Vision & Policy** | Government policy | RBI's 2012 Payment Systems Vision document; PMJDY financial inclusion drive |
| **L2 — Regulation** | Regulatory framework | RBI as regulator; Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007; NPCI as recognised payment system operator |
| **L3 — Digital Rails / Infrastructure** | Core infrastructure | NPCI's UPI switch; IMPS settlement layer; National Financial Switch (NFS) |
| **L4 — APIs & Protocols** | Open standards | UPI API (open, published by NPCI); interoperable VPA/QR standards; Bharat QR |
| **L5 — Applications** | End-user apps | PSP bank apps (BHIM, YONO, iMobile); TPAP apps (PhonePe, GPay, Paytm) |
| **L6 — Use Cases** | Sectoral applications | P2P transfers, P2M payments, bill payments, AutoPay mandates, Credit on UPI, cross-border |
| **L7 — Analytics & Intelligence** | Data & insights | Transaction analytics, fraud monitoring, NPCI's data outputs to RBI and government |

UPI is the quintessential **L3-L4 DPI component** — it provides the foundational digital rails and open APIs upon which an entire ecosystem of applications and use cases has been built.

## Regulatory Framework

### Primary Legislation

- **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007** — The overarching legal framework governing all payment systems in India, including UPI. Gives RBI the authority to regulate and supervise payment systems. [^13]
- **Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934** — Establishes RBI's mandate for monetary policy and payment system oversight.
- **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** — India's first comprehensive data protection law, applicable to UPI transaction data processed by banks, TPAPs, and NPCI. Penalties up to **₹250 crore per contravention**. [^14]
- **Information Technology Act, 2000** — Provides the baseline for electronic transactions and cybercrime provisions.

### Key Regulatory Directives

| Regulation | Details |
|---|---|
| **RBI Data Localisation (April 2018)** | All payment system data must be stored exclusively in India. No payment data can be stored or processed offshore without explicit RBI approval. [^15] |
| **Zero MDR on UPI (January 2020)** | Government mandated zero Merchant Discount Rate on UPI and RuPay debit cards to promote digital payments. Consumers pay nothing; merchants pay nothing for bank-account-based UPI. Interchange fees apply only to PPI-wallet-funded UPI transactions above ₹2,000 (up to 1.1%). [^16] |
| **RBI Discussion Paper on Charges (August 2022)** | RBI sought public comments on whether charges should be levied on UPI transactions. The Finance Ministry rejected the proposal, declaring UPI a "digital public good" and stating cost recovery must come from other means. [^17] |
| **RBI Online Dispute Resolution (August 2020)** | Mandated payment system operators to implement ODR for customer complaints. NPCI responded with the Unified Dispute & Issue Resolution (UDIR) framework. [^18] |
| **NPCI 30% Volume Cap (November 2020)** | No single TPAP may process more than 30% of total UPI transaction volume. Extended three times — current deadline: **31 December 2026**. As of April 2026, PhonePe held 47.07% and Google Pay ~37%, far exceeding the cap. [^19] |
| **P2P Collect Discontinued (October 2025)** | NPCI removed the P2P collect request feature to combat fraud. [^20] |
| **Beneficiary Name Display (June 2025)** | UPI apps must show the beneficiary's bank-registered name via CBS integration before payment confirmation. [^21] |
| **P2M Limit Enhancement (September 2025)** | NPCI raised P2M limits to ₹10 lakh/day for verified merchants in specific categories (capital markets, insurance, travel, loan repayment, tax payments). P2P limit remains at ₹1 lakh/day. [^22] |
| **RBI Delegates P2M Limits to NPCI (April 2025)** | RBI empowered NPCI to set and revise P2M transaction limits in consultation with stakeholders, while keeping P2P cap at ₹1 lakh unchanged. [^23] |
| **DPDP Rules (November 2025)** | The DPDP Rules, 2025 operationalise the DPDP Act 2023, prescribing data governance, consent management, and compliance timelines for fintech including UPI ecosystem participants. [^24] |

## Citizen Rights Analysis

### What Works for Citizens

**1. Zero-cost payments.** The zero MDR policy means citizens pay nothing to send or receive money via UPI — whether P2P or P2M. This is a genuine public good and has been the single biggest driver of adoption, especially for small merchants who were previously excluded from digital payments due to card MDR costs. [^16]

**2. Interoperability.** Unlike card networks where merchant acceptance depends on the network, any UPI app works with any other. A merchant with a single QR code can accept payments from all 741 banks and all TPAP apps. This eliminates lock-in and gives consumers genuine choice. [^3]

**3. 24/7 availability.** Unlike NEFT (which had batch processing windows before 2019) or RTGS (which has operating hours), UPI is available round-the-clock, including weekends and holidays. This is a meaningful right for citizens who need to make emergency payments at any time.

**4. Financial inclusion.** UPI 123PAY (feature phones, no internet), UPI Lite (low connectivity, small payments), and the PMJDY-UPI integration have brought digital payments to populations that were entirely excluded from formal financial systems. Over **55.98 crore Jan Dhan accounts** carry RuPay debit cards linked to UPI. [^8][^25]

**5. Instant settlement.** Unlike cheques (days), NEFT (hours in batches), or card refunds (3-7 business days), UPI settles in real-time. Failed transactions are reversed automatically. This gives citizens immediate certainty about their money.

### Concerns for Citizens

**1. Monopolistic concentration at the app layer.** Despite NPCI's 30% volume cap (in place since November 2020, deadline now December 2026), the market remains a **de facto duopoly**: PhonePe (~47%) and Google Pay (~37%) together process over **84% of all UPI transactions** as of mid-2026. The cap has been extended three times without enforcement, raising questions about systemic risk, data concentration, and the ability of citizens to avoid two private corporations for essential payment infrastructure. [^19]

**2. Fraud is rising in absolute terms.** UPI fraud incidents reached **6.32 lakh cases valued at ₹485 crore in FY2024-25** (up from 7.25 lakh cases in FY2022-23). While the fraud rate as a percentage of total transaction value remains low (~0.4%), the absolute numbers are large and growing. Approximately **1 in 5 UPI users** have experienced fraud, and **51% of victims do not file complaints** — suggesting redressal mechanisms are either inaccessible or ineffective for many citizens. [^12]

**3. No explicit data protection for transaction metadata.** While the DPDP Act 2023 now provides a statutory framework, UPI transaction data — amounts, timestamps, merchant identities, frequency patterns — constitutes a detailed behavioural profile of every citizen. NPCI, banks, and TPAPs all process this data. The question of who owns this data, how long it is retained, and for what secondary purposes it can be used remains inadequately addressed in citizen-facing terms. [^14][^26]

**4. Cost recovery ambiguity threatens long-term sustainability.** The zero MDR policy means the full cost of operating UPI — infrastructure, security, fraud prevention, settlement — is borne by banks and NPCI. The Finance Ministry has declared UPI a "digital public good" but has not clarified who funds its operating costs long-term. The RBI's 2022 discussion paper on charges was rejected, but the underlying fiscal question remains unresolved. Citizens benefit from zero cost today, but opacity about cost recovery creates uncertainty about future policy changes. [^17]

**5. P2P collect removal reduces functionality.** The discontinuation of P2P collect requests (October 2025) removed a legitimate use case — freelancers, small businesses, and informal workers who relied on payment requests from clients must now share their UPI ID or QR code. While the move was justified by fraud concerns, it was a blunt instrument that reduced citizen utility without offering an alternative. [^20]

## Privacy Implications

### What Data Does UPI Generate?

Every UPI transaction generates the following data points, processed and stored across multiple entities:

- **Sender and receiver VPA / bank account details**
- **Transaction amount and timestamp**
- **Merchant category and identity** (for P2M)
- **Device information** (app, OS, device type)
- **IP address / geolocation** (in many cases)
- **Transaction pattern data** (frequency, merchant preferences, spending categories)

### Data Flow and Retention

```
User → TPAP App → PSP Bank → NPCI Switch → Beneficiary Bank → Beneficiary
```

- **TPAPs** (PhonePe, GPay, etc.) collect device data, app usage patterns, and in some cases behavioural analytics. Their privacy policies govern this data, but enforcement is inconsistent.
- **PSP Banks** store transaction records as per RBI's retention requirements (minimum 5-8 years depending on the regulation). [^27]
- **NPCI** processes and routes transaction data but claims not to store personal transaction data beyond operational requirements.
- **RBI Data Localisation mandate** (April 2018) requires all payment data to be stored in India. [^15]

### DPDP Act 2023 Implications

The DPDP Act classifies financial transaction data as personal data and, in many cases, as **significant personal data** (though the final rules are still being operationalised). Key implications: [^14][^24]

- **Consent**: Users must consent to data processing. However, the purpose limitation for UPI data is broad ("payment processing"), and the Act allows processing for "compliance with law" — which covers AML, KYC, and fraud detection.
- **Right to erasure**: Citizens can request data deletion, but this conflicts with RBI's mandatory record retention requirements. Banks must hold transaction records for specified periods — the DPDP Act's Section 17(2) provides that sectoral laws (like RBI regulations) will prevail in case of conflict. [^14]
- **Data principal rights**: Users have the right to access, correct, and nominate their data. Whether these rights are meaningfully exercisable against NPCI or TPAPs for UPI transaction data remains to be tested.
- **Penalties**: Up to ₹250 crore per contravention — significantly higher than RBI's penalty powers, creating a new compliance dimension. [^14]

### Surveillance Risk

UPI transaction data, when aggregated, creates a **comprehensive financial profile** of every citizen: where they shop, what they buy, how often they travel, who they pay, what services they subscribe to. While this data is held by private entities (banks, TPAPs) under regulatory oversight, the legal framework for:

- **Government access** to UPI transaction data (via court orders, law enforcement requests)
- **Data sharing** between NPCI, banks, and government agencies for purposes beyond payment processing
- **Analytics and AI-driven profiling** by TPAPs using transaction patterns

...remains less transparent than citizens may assume. The CSIS has noted that "UPI illustrates how financial infrastructure doubles as data infrastructure, requiring regulatory frameworks capable of governing sensitive behavioural data at scale." [^26]

## Safeguards

### Technical Safeguards

- **Two-factor authentication**: UPI requires both device binding (the registered mobile number) and a UPI PIN or biometric authentication for every transaction
- **Device binding**: NPCI has progressively tightened device-binding requirements to prevent unauthorised access
- **35-digit alphanumeric transaction IDs** (mandatory since February 2025): Standardised transaction identifiers for traceability and audit [^28]
- **Encryption**: End-to-end encryption for data in transit between all participants
- **Beneficiary name display** (mandatory since June 2025): Apps must show the recipient's bank-registered name before confirming payment [^21]
- **P2P collect discontinued** (October 2025): Removed a major fraud vector [^20]
- **International QR "share and pay" disabled**: Prevents remote scanning of shared QR codes by international actors [^21]

### Regulatory Safeguards

- **RBI supervision**: UPI operates under RBI's regulatory framework; RBI can issue directives, impose penalties, and mandate changes
- **NPCI governance**: As a not-for-profit owned by banks, NPCI is mandated to operate in the interest of the payment ecosystem rather than for shareholder profit
- **Data localisation**: All payment system data must remain in India [^15]
- **DPDP Act 2023**: Statutory data protection with penalties up to ₹250 crore [^14]
- **RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021**: Free grievance redressal for all RBI-regulated entities [^29]
- **UDIR framework**: NPCI's Unified Dispute & Issue Resolution system for in-app complaint handling [^18]
- **30% volume cap**: Intended to prevent single-point-of-failure risk at the TPAP layer (though unenforced as of August 2026) [^19]

## Complaints & Grievance Redressal

Citizens facing UPI issues have a **three-tier escalation path**:

### Tier 1: In-App Complaint
Raise a complaint through the UPI app (TPAP or PSP bank app). Under the UDIR framework, TPAPs must provide in-app dispute resolution mechanisms. NPCI circulars (November 2020, October 2021, April 2022, ongoing) mandate that apps display complaint options for every transaction. [^18]

### Tier 2: NPCI Dispute Redressal Portal
If the TPAP/PSP does not resolve the issue, complainants can escalate to NPCI's online portal: [https://npci.org.in/upi-complaint](https://npci.org.in/upi-complaint). Select UPI as the product, fill in transaction details, and NPCI investigates. [^30]

### Tier 3: RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021
If NPCI does not resolve the complaint within 30 days, citizens can escalate to the **RBI Integrated Ombudsman** — completely free of charge. [^29]

- **Online portal**: [https://cms.rbi.org.in](https://cms.rbi.org.in)
- **Toll-free number**: **14448** (available in Hindi, English, and 10 regional languages)
- **Email**: cpc@rbi.org.in
- **Physical**: Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre, 4th Floor, Sector 17, Chandigarh - 160017

The Ombudsman has 22 regional offices ensuring territorial accessibility. Complaints must be filed with the office having jurisdiction over the bank branch where the account is held or where the disputed transaction occurred. [^29]

### Key Concern
Research by Dvara Research found that **51% of UPI fraud victims do not file complaints**, indicating that while formal mechanisms exist, they are **not effectively reaching or serving all citizens**, particularly those in lower-income, lower-literacy, or rural demographics. [^12]

## International Expansion

UPI's internationalisation is managed by **NPCI International Payments Limited (NIPL)**, a subsidiary of NPCI. As of August 2026, UPI has linkages in **10+ countries**: [^10][^11]

| Country | Linkage Type | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Singapore** | UPI-PayNow | Real-time cross-border remittances; 19 Indian banks linked as of July 2025; world's first cloud-based real-time cross-border payment system |
| **UAE** | UPI-AaniNet | Indian tourists can pay at merchants via UPI QR |
| **Bhutan** | UPI-Bhutan QR | Cross-border merchant payments |
| **Nepal** | UPI-NepalPay | Cross-border remittances and merchant payments |
| **Sri Lanka** | UPI-LankaPay | Merchant payment acceptance |
| **France** | UPI-Eurogiro | First European country with UPI acceptance |
| **Mauritius** | UPI-MTC | Merchant payment acceptance |
| **Qatar** | UPI-QPay | Merchant payment acceptance |
| **Oman** | UPI-OmanNet | Merchant payment acceptance |
| **Malaysia** | UPI-DuitNow (agreement signed Feb 2026) | QR-based cross-border merchant payments between India and Malaysia; phased rollout |

Additionally, NIPL has signed agreements for UPI expansion to **Greece** and is exploring linkages with several other countries including Japan (via NTT DATA MoU). [^10][^11]
